The Festival Wheel

Hindu festivals are fixed by the Moon, not the Gregorian calendar, so they move from year to year. Tied to the Panchanga, they keep their place in the lunar year. The wheel below lays out that year: twelve months, each with a bright half and a dark half, six seasons, and each festival on its tithi.

How to read the wheel

A tithi is a lunar day

Each tithi is the time the Moon takes to gain 12° on the Sun. Thirty tithis make a lunar month, so the wheel has 360 of them, one degree each. Tithi is one of the five limbs of the Panchanga.

Bright half and dark half

Shukla paksha runs from new moon to full moon, the waxing half. Krishna paksha runs from full moon back to new moon. Most festivals are named by month, paksha and tithi, for example Chaitra Shukla Navami for Rama Navami.

Where the month ends

This wheel uses amanta months, ending at the new moon, as in South India, Maharashtra and Gujarat. Most of North India uses purnimanta months that end at the full moon. There, a dark-half festival takes the next month's name: Janmashtami becomes Bhadrapada Krishna Ashtami, but the day itself is the same.

Keeping pace with the Sun

Twelve lunar months fall about eleven days short of a solar year. Roughly every thirty-two and a half months an extra month (adhika masa) is added. Solar festivals such as Makara Sankranti follow the Sun's entry into a sign instead, so they land on a different tithi each year.

The festivals, month by month

1

Ugadi · Gudi Padwa युगादि

Chaitra · Shukla Pratipada

The lunar New Year in the Deccan: Ugadi in Karnataka, Andhra and Telangana, Gudi Padwa in Maharashtra. A new Samvatsara of the sixty-year cycle begins, and families taste neem with jaggery, bitter with sweet.

KarnatakaAndhraMaharashtra
2

Rama Navami राम नवमी

Chaitra · Shukla Navami

The birth of Rama at noon in Ayodhya. Many keep the nine days before it as Vasanta Navaratri. See the Ramayana journey.

Pan-Indian
3

Hanuman Jayanti हनुमान जयन्ती

Chaitra · Shukla Purnima

Kept on Chaitra Purnima across much of North India. Regional calendars differ: Tamil Nadu keeps it on the new moon of Margazhi, and Andhra in Vaishakha.

North India
4

Mesha Sankranti मेष संक्रान्ति

Solar · the Sun enters Mesha (Aries)

The Sun enters Aries, usually about 14 April. It is the solar New Year in many regions: Puthandu in Tamil Nadu, Vishu in Kerala, Baisakhi in Punjab, Bohag Bihu in Assam and Pohela Boishakh in Bengal.

Tamil NaduKeralaPunjabAssamBengal
5

Akshaya Tritiya अक्षय तृतीया

Vaishakha · Shukla Tritiya

The "imperishable" third. Whatever is begun or given on this day is believed to grow without end, so it is a favourite day for weddings, new ventures and buying gold.

Pan-Indian
6

Narasimha Jayanti नृसिंह जयन्ती

Vaishakha · Shukla Chaturdashi

The day the man-lion burst from the pillar to save Prahlada. See the Dashavatara.

AhobilamSimhachalam
7

Ganga Dussehra गङ्गा दशहरा

Jyeshtha · Shukla Dashami

The descent of the Ganga to earth through Bhagirathas penance, marked with bathing at Haridwar and Varanasi.

North India
8

Vat Purnima वट पूर्णिमा

Jyeshtha · Shukla Purnima

Married women tie threads around a banyan tree in memory of Savitri, who won back her husband Satyavan from Yama. North India keeps the same vow, Vat Savitri, on the new moon of Jyeshtha instead.

MaharashtraGujaratKarnataka
9

Rath Yatra रथ यात्रा

Ashadha · Shukla Dvitiya

Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra ride out of the Puri temple on three giant wooden chariots, rebuilt every year.

PuriOdisha
10

Ashadhi Ekadashi आषाढी एकादशी

Ashadha · Shukla Ekadashi

Vishnu lies down to sleep on the serpent Shesha (Devshayani Ekadashi), and the four months of Chaturmasya begin. The great Wari pilgrimage on foot reaches Vithoba at Pandharpur on this day.

PandharpurMaharashtra
11

Guru Purnima गुरु पूर्णिमा

Ashadha · Shukla Purnima

Vyasa Purnima, the day to honour ones teachers. Sannyasis begin their rainy-season retreat. See the guru paramparas.

Pan-Indian
12

Naga Panchami नाग पञ्चमी

Shravana · Shukla Panchami

Serpent deities are worshipped with milk at anthills and nagakal stones as the monsoon brings snakes out of the earth.

Pan-Indian
13

Raksha Bandhan · Upakarma रक्षाबन्धन · उपाकर्म

Shravana · Shukla Purnima

Sisters tie the protective rakhi on their brothers wrists. On the same full moon, Yajurvedi Brahmins renew the sacred thread (Avani Avittam in Tamil Nadu).

Pan-Indian
14

Krishna Janmashtami कृष्ण जन्माष्टमी

Shravana · Krishna Ashtami

Krishnas birth at midnight. Here it falls in Shravana; calendars that end the month at the full moon call the same day Bhadrapada Krishna Ashtami. Dahi Handi follows in Maharashtra.

Pan-IndianMathuraGokulashtami in the South
15

Ganesh Chaturthi गणेश चतुर्थी

Bhadrapada · Shukla Chaturthi

Clay images of Ganesha are installed at home and in public pandals, worshipped for one to ten days and then immersed in water.

MaharashtraKarnatakaTelanganaPan-Indian
16

Mahalaya Amavasya महालय अमावास्या

Bhadrapada · Amavasya (new moon)

The last day of Pitru Paksha, the fortnight for offerings to ancestors. In Bengal it also heralds Durga Puja.

Pan-IndianBengal
17

Navaratri नवरात्रि

Ashvina · Shukla Pratipada

Nine nights of the Goddess. Bengal keeps them as Durga Puja, Gujarat dances garba, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka set up golu displays of dolls, and Mysuru holds its Dasara.

Pan-Indian
18

Vijayadashami · Dussehra विजयादशमी

Ashvina · Shukla Dashami

Victory day: Rama over Ravana in the North, Durga over Mahishasura in the East. It is an auspicious day to begin learning (Vidyarambham).

Pan-Indian
19

Deepavali दीपावली

Ashvina · Amavasya (new moon)

The festival of lamps. Lakshmi Puja falls on the new moon, and the South celebrates Naraka Chaturdashi, Krishnas victory over Narakasura, at dawn the day before. Northern calendars call the month Kartika.

Pan-Indian
20

Prabodhini Ekadashi प्रबोधिनी एकादशी

Kartika · Shukla Ekadashi

Vishnu wakes from his four-month sleep and Chaturmasya ends. Tulasi Vivah, the marriage of the tulasi plant to Vishnu, follows.

Pan-Indian
21

Kartika Purnima · Karthigai Deepam कार्तिक पूर्णिमा

Kartika · Shukla Purnima

The Ganga ghats of Varanasi glow for Dev Deepavali. In Tamil Nadu the great beacon is lit on Arunachala at Tiruvannamalai, timed by the Krittika star in the month of Karthigai.

VaranasiTiruvannamalai
22

Gita Jayanti गीता जयन्ती

Margashirsha · Shukla Ekadashi

The day Krishna spoke the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna at Kurukshetra, kept on Mokshada Ekadashi.

KurukshetraPan-Indian
23

Vaikuntha Ekadashi वैकुण्ठ एकादशी

Pausha · Shukla Ekadashi

The "gate to Vaikuntha" (Paramapada Vasal) opens in Vishnu temples such as Srirangam and Tirumala. It falls in the solar month of Margazhi (Dhanu), so its lunar month varies, and it is shown here at its usual place.

SrirangamTirumala
24

Makara Sankranti · Pongal मकर सङ्क्रान्ति

Solar · the Sun enters Makara (Capricorn)

The Sun enters Capricorn and begins its northward course (Uttarayana), usually about 14 January. It is Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Lohri the evening before in Punjab, Magh Bihu in Assam, and the kite festival Uttarayan in Gujarat.

Tamil NaduGujaratPunjabAssam
25

Vasant Panchami वसन्त पञ्चमी

Magha · Shukla Panchami

The first breath of spring and the day of Saraswati. Children write their first letters, and books and instruments are worshipped.

Pan-IndianBengal
26

Ratha Saptami रथ सप्तमी

Magha · Shukla Saptami

Surya turns his chariot northward. It is a bathing festival for the Sun god, with arka leaves placed on the head.

South India
27

Maha Shivaratri महाशिवरात्रि

Magha · Krishna Chaturdashi

The great night of Shiva, kept with fasting and a vigil of four night-long worships. Here it falls in Magha; calendars that end the month at the full moon call it Phalguna. See Shiva & Muruga.

Pan-Indian
28

Holi · Panguni Uttiram होली

Phalguna · Shukla Purnima

Bonfires for Holika on the full-moon night, then a day of colours. In Tamil Nadu the same full moon is Panguni Uttiram, when the divine weddings of Shiva and Parvati and of Murugan and Deivanai are celebrated.

North IndiaTamil Nadu
