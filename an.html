Azhwars & Nayanmars

Two parallel streams of Tamil Bhakti — the Vaishnava Azhwars immersed in love of Vishnu, and the Saiva Nayanmars devoted to Shiva. Together they shaped the devotional landscape of South India from the 6th to 9th centuries CE.

Vaishnava (Azhwars) Saiva (Nayanmars)

🔵 The Twelve Azhwars (Vaishnava)

The Azhwars ("those immersed in God") sang in Tamil of their love for Vishnu. Their hymns were collected as the Nalayira Divya Prabandham (Four Thousand Divine Verses), revered by Sri Vaishnavas as the Tamil Veda.

c. 7th century CE

Poigai Azhwar பொய்கையாழ்வார்

Incarnation of Vishnu's conch

One of the three earliest Azhwars, born in Kanchipuram. His Mudhal Thiruvandhadhi opens the Divya Prabandham canon.

Mudhal Thiruvandhadhi (100 verses)
c. 7th century CE

Bhoothath Azhwar பூதத்தாழ்வார்

Incarnation of Vishnu's mace

Born in Mahabalipuram. His hymns express wonder at Vishnu's presence in all things, forming the second poem of the canon.

Irandam Thiruvandhadhi (100 verses)
c. 7th century CE

Pey Azhwar பேயாழ்வார்

Incarnation of Vishnu's sword

Born in Mylapore. Tradition says the three earliest Azhwars met miraculously, and their combined devotion lit a lamp that became the light of Vaishnavism.

Moondram Thiruvandhadhi (100 verses)
c. 7th century CE

Thirumazhisai Azhwar திருமழிசையாழ்வார்

Incarnation of Vishnu's discus

A former Jain who converted to Vaishnavism. His works blend philosophical inquiry with devotional surrender.

Nanmugan Thiruvandhadhi (96)Thiruchanda Virutham (120)
c. 8th century CE

Nammalvar நம்மாழ்வார்

The greatest of the Azhwars

Considered the most important Azhwar. His Tiruvaymoli is called the "Tamil Veda" and is praised as the essence of the Upanishads in Tamil.

Tiruvaymoli (1,102 verses)Tiruviruttam (100)
c. 8th century CE

Madhurakavi Azhwar மதுரகவியாழ்வார்

Disciple of Nammalvar

His entire surviving work is a hymn of devotion to his guru Nammalvar — a rare celebration of the teacher as the path to God.

Kanninun Siruthambu (11 verses)
c. 9th century CE

Kulasekhara Azhwar குலசேகராழ்வார்

A Chera king who became a saint

A ruling king who renounced his throne for devotion. His Perumal Tirumoli expresses the anguish of separation from Vishnu.

Perumal Tirumoli (105 verses)
c. 8th century CE

Periyalvar பெரியாழ்வார்

Foster father of Andal

Worshipped Vishnu as a mother loves her child Krishna. He discovered the baby Andal in his tulsi garden and raised her in devotion.

Periyalvar Tirumoli (461 verses)Tiruppallandu (12)
c. 8th century CE

Andal ஆண்டாள் ♀

The only female Azhwar

Raised by Periyalvar, she refused all earthly marriage and merged with Vishnu at Srirangam. Her Tiruppavai is recited in Vaishnava homes every December.

Tiruppavai (30 verses)Nachiyar Tirumoli (143)
c. 8th century CE

Thondaradippodi Azhwar தொண்டரடிப்பொடியாழ்வார்

The humble servant

A former pleasure-seeker who reformed through devotion. He expresses deep humility and the joy of service to Vishnu's devotees.

Tirumalai (45 verses)Tirupalliezhuchi (10)
c. 8th century CE

Thiruppaan Azhwar திருப்பாணாழ்வார்

From a marginalized community

A saint from a low-caste background whose devotion transcended social barriers. His ten verses on the beauty of Ranganatha are celebrated for their ecstatic vision.

Amalanadhipiran (10 verses)
c. 8th century CE

Thirumangai Azhwar திருமங்கையாழ்வார்

The last of the twelve

A chieftain-turned-saint who composed the most verses after Nammalvar. His Periya Tirumoli is a vast tour of Vishnu's shrines across Tamil Nadu.

Periya Tirumoli (1,084 verses)Tirunedunthandakam (30)

🔴 Key Nayanmars (Saiva)

The Nayanmars ("servants of Shiva") number 63. The list begins with Sundarar's Tiruthondar Thogai, was elaborated by Nambiyandar Nambi, and was told in full in Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. The four most prominent — Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar and Manikkavacakar — are called the Nalvar, and their hymns form the core of the Tirumurai.

c. 580–661 CE

Appar (Thirunavukkarasar) அப்பர்

One of the Nalvar

Originally a Jain who converted back to Saivism through his sister's influence. He travelled tirelessly to Shiva temples, and his hymns (over 3,000 verses) are among the earliest in the Tevaram.

Tevaram (Books 4–6)
c. 635–651 CE

Sambandar (Thirugnanasambandar) சம்பந்தர்

Child saint of the Nalvar

A Brahmin child prodigy who began singing Shiva's praises at age three. His intense, joyful hymns form the first three books of the Tevaram and are considered the foundation of Tamil Saiva devotion.

Tevaram (Books 1–3)
c. 8th century CE

Sundarar (Sundaramurti) சுந்தரர்

The third of the Nalvar

A friend of Shiva who sang with startling intimacy — treating the Lord as a companion and even demanding favors. His Tiruthondar Thogai is the earliest list of the 63 Nayanmars.

Tevaram (Book 7)Tiruthondar Thogai
c. 9th century CE

Manikkavacakar மாணிக்கவாசகர்

The fourth of the Nalvar

A minister who became a saint. His Tiruvachakam is considered the most emotionally profound Saiva text, exploring the soul's longing and surrender to Shiva. He is not counted among the 63 Nayanmars, but is revered as one of the Nalvar.

Tiruvachakam (Book 8)
c. 6th century CE

Karaikkal Ammaiyar காரைக்கால் அம்மையார் ♀

The earliest woman Nayanmar

One of the earliest Saiva saints and one of the three women among the 63 Nayanmars (with Mangayarkkarasiyar and Isaignaniyar). She prayed to become a ghost so she could serve Shiva without worldly attachments, and her hymns are fierce and otherworldly.

Tirumurai (Book 11)
Date uncertain (legendary)

Kannappa Nayanar கண்ணப்பர்

The hunter-devotee

A tribal hunter whose love for Shiva was so pure that he offered his own eyes to stop the deity's bleeding. His story embodies the Nayanmar ideal: devotion beyond ritual purity.

Periya Puranam

📚 The Two Canons

Nalayira Divya Prabandham

The Vaishnava Canon

Four thousand Tamil verses by the twelve Azhwars, compiled by Nathamuni in the 9th century. Revered as the "Tamil Veda" by Sri Vaishnavas, it is recited in temples and homes to this day.

4,000 verses12 Azhwars

Tirumurai

The Saiva Canon

Twelve books of Tamil Saiva hymns. Nambiyandar Nambi compiled the first eleven, traditionally under Raja Raja Chola I; Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam was added later as the twelfth. The first seven books contain the Tevaram hymns of Appar, Sambandar and Sundarar.

12 volumesTevaram core
