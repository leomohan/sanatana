Arupadai Veedu

The six sacred abodes of Lord Muruga, sung in the ancient Tamil Sangam poem Thirumurugatruppadai by Nakkirar. Each temple marks a chapter in Muruga's divine life — from his marriage to his victory over evil, from his wisdom to his renunciation.

Six Abodes of Muruga
1

Thiruparankundram திருப்பரங்குன்றம்

The Wedding Abode

Carved into a hillock near Madurai, this is where Muruga married Deivanai, the daughter of Indra. It is the first of the six abodes and a rare rock-cut temple where the sanctum is hewn from living stone.

💍 WeddingMadurai
2

Thiruchendur திருச்செந்தூர்

The Victory Abode

The only abode on the seashore. Here Muruga defeated the demon Surapadma after a fierce battle, and worshipped Shiva in gratitude. The temple stands just 200 metres from the Gulf of Mannar, yet is said never to flood.

⚔️ VictoryCoastalSkanda Sashti
3

Palani பழனி

The Abode of Renunciation

After losing the contest for the sacred fruit to his brother Ganesha, Muruga left his family in anger and settled on these hills as an ascetic. Here he is worshipped as Dhandayuthapani — the Lord with a staff, wearing only a loincloth.

🧘 RenunciationHilltopNavapashanam idol
4

Swamimalai சுவாமிமலை

The Abode of Wisdom

At this hillock near Kumbakonam, the child Muruga taught the meaning of the Pranava Mantra (Om) to his own father, Shiva — and demanded that Shiva accept him as guru. Sixty steps lead to the shrine, one for each year of the Hindu calendar cycle.

🧠 WisdomGuru of Shiva60 steps
5

Thiruthani திருத்தணி

The Abode of Union

On this hill near Chennai, Muruga married Valli, the daughter of a tribal chieftain — a union celebrated as the blending of divine grace with the devotion of the humble. Its great festival is Aadi Krithigai, and pilgrims climb its 365 steps on New Year's Eve.

💞 UnionValliAadi Krithigai
6

Pazhamudircholai பழமுதிர்சோலை

The Forest Abode

Nestled in the Azhagar Kovil forests near Madurai, this is the last of the six abodes. Here Muruga tested the Tamil saint Avvaiyar with a fruit, revealing his playful wisdom. It is a serene forest shrine, far from the bustle of the other temples.
