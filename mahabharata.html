The people who drove the story

Bhishma भीष्म

The grandsire

Born Devavrata, the eighth son of Ganga. He gave up the throne and marriage so that his father could marry Satyavati. For that terrible vow (bhishma) he got his name, and the boon of choosing the hour of his death. He fell on the tenth day of the war. From his bed of arrows he taught Yudhishthira the long lessons of the Shanti and Anushasana Parvas.

Vow of celibacyBed of arrowsVishnu Sahasranama

Satyavati & Vyasa सत्यवती · व्यास

Mother and son

Satyavati, a fisher chief's daughter, bore Vyasa to the sage Parashara on an island in the Yamuna; hence his name Dvaipayana. When her sons by Shantanu died without heirs, she called Vyasa to father children with the widows. Vyasa is also the epic's author. Tradition says Ganesha wrote it down as he dictated.

NiyogaAuthor of the epic

Dhritarashtra & Gandhari धृतराष्ट्र · गान्धारी

The blind king and his queen

Dhritarashtra, born blind, was passed over for his brother Pandu. When Pandu died he ruled. Gandhari of Gandhara bound her own eyes for life to share her husband's darkness. Her hundred sons and a daughter came from a mass of flesh that Vyasa divided into pots.

Duryodhana दुर्योधन

Eldest of the Kauravas

Duryodhana believed the throne was his by right. He feared Bhima's strength and resented the Pandavas' rise. He made Karna a king and his closest friend. With Shakuni's loaded dice he won everything from Yudhishthira. Bhima broke his thigh with a mace on the eighteenth day.

The dice gameFriend of Karna

Pandu, Kunti & Madri पाण्डु · कुन्ती · माद्री

Parents of the Pandavas

A sage whom Pandu shot while the sage was in a deer's form cursed him to die if he lay with a wife. Kunti held a mantra from the sage Durvasa that could summon any god. Through it she bore Yudhishthira by Dharma, Bhima by Vayu and Arjuna by Indra. She shared the mantra with Madri, who bore the twins by the Ashvins.

Durvasa's mantraFive sons

The five Pandavas पञ्च पाण्डव

Sons of Pandu

Raised in the forest and brought to Hastinapura as boys, the five shared everything, including their wife. They lost their kingdom at dice and spent twelve years in the forest and a thirteenth in disguise. Then they won the war at Kurukshetra.

Karna कर्ण

The first-born no one knew

Kunti's son by the Sun, born before her marriage and set adrift in a basket. A charioteer's family raised him. Mocked for his birth, he found a friend in Duryodhana and never broke with him. He gave away his divine armour and earrings to Indra, who came disguised as a brahmin. Arjuna killed him on the seventeenth day.

Kavacha & kundalaKing of AngaGenerosity

Draupadi द्रौपदी

Born of fire

Draupadi rose with her brother Dhrishtadyumna from Drupada's sacrificial fire. Arjuna won her by hitting the target at her swayamvara, and Kunti's word made her wife to all five brothers. Dragged into the dice hall, she asked whether a man who had lost himself could still stake her. Her garment was miraculously replenished; later tellings credit Krishna.

SwayamvaraThe dice hallPanchakanya

Krishna कृष्ण

Cousin, counsellor, charioteer

Krishna was the son of Vasudeva, Kunti's brother, which made him the Pandavas' cousin. Arjuna was also his friend and Subhadra's husband. He went as the Pandavas' envoy to Hastinapura and would not bear arms in the war. As Arjuna's charioteer he spoke the Bhagavad Gita. He is the eighth avatar in most lists.

Envoy of peaceCharioteerThe Gita

Abhimanyu & Parikshit अभिमन्यु · परीक्षित्

The line that survived

Abhimanyu, Arjuna's son by Subhadra, broke into Drona's wheel formation on the thirteenth day and was killed inside it. In the popular telling he had learned to enter it while still in the womb. His son Parikshit was struck in the womb by Ashwatthama's weapon, and Krishna revived him. Parikshit became the Pandavas' only heir.

ChakravyuhaThe surviving heir

Vidura विदुर

The voice of dharma

Vidura was the third son by Vyasa, born to a maid. A curse had made Dharma himself take birth as him. As Dhritarashtra's minister he warned against the dice game, and he saved the Pandavas from the house of lac. His counsel to the king is known as the Vidura Niti.

House of lacVidura Niti

Drona & Ashwatthama द्रोण · अश्वत्थामा

Teacher and son

Drona, born from a vessel (drona) of the sage Bharadvaja, taught arms to all the Kuru princes. Arjuna was his favourite, and he demanded Ekalavya's thumb. He fought for the Kauravas. When told falsely that Ashwatthama was dead, he laid down his weapons, and Dhrishtadyumna killed him. In revenge Ashwatthama slaughtered the sleeping Pandava camp, including the five sons of Draupadi.
