Jyotisha

The architecture of Vedic time and sky — the nine Grahas that rule, the twelve Rashis and twenty-seven Nakshatras they govern, the five limbs of the Panchanga that organise daily life, and the sixty-year Samvatsara cycle that turns the long wheel.

🪐 The Nine Grahas (Planetary Rulers)

The Navagraha are the governors of the sky — every Rashi and Nakshatra is ruled by one of them. They are worshipped as deities and their positions shape the birth chart (Kundali).

📅 The Panchanga (Five Limbs of Time)

The Panchanga is the traditional Hindu almanac — its name means "five limbs" because it tracks five aspects of time based on the Moon's motion. Nakshatra is one of the five; the others are how people use the sky every day. [citation:4][citation:9]

Tithi तिथि

Lunar Day

The time for the Moon-Sun longitude gap to grow by 12°. There are 30 Tithis in a lunar month, divided into Shukla Paksha (waxing) and Krishna Paksha (waning). [citation:14]

Vara वार

Weekday

The seven days of the week, each ruled by a Graha: Ravi (Sun), Soma (Moon), Mangala (Mars), Budha (Mercury), Guru (Jupiter), Shukra (Venus), Shani (Saturn).

Nakshatra नक्षत्र

Lunar Mansion

The Moon's transit through one of the 27 lunar mansions on a given day. Used for naming children, scheduling ceremonies, and matching compatibility. [citation:9]

Yoga योग

Sun-Moon Combination

A calculation based on the sum of the Sun and Moon's longitudes. There are 27 Yogas; some are considered auspicious and others inauspicious for specific activities. [citation:14]

Karana करण

Half-Tithi

Half of a lunar day; two Karanas per Tithi. There are 11 Karanas in total. The Vishti (Bhadra) Karana is avoided for auspicious ceremonies. [citation:4][citation:14]

🔄 The Sixty Samvatsaras (Jovian Year Cycle)

A cycle of 60 named years, traditionally linked to Jupiter's movement. The names repeat every 60 years, divided into three groups of 20 attributed to Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva. Regional lists vary slightly in spelling. [citation:5][citation:10]
