The Mathas

Two great lineages of monastic institutions: the four Amnaya Peethams established by Adi Shankara for Advaita Vedanta, and the eight Ashta Mathas established by Madhvacharya for Dvaita Vedanta at Udupi.

Sringeri Sharada Peetham शृंगेरी शारदा पीठम्

Dakshinamnaya · South

The southern seat, located on the banks of the Tunga River in Karnataka. Dedicated to Goddess Sharadamba, it is the most prominent of the four Peethams and has an unbroken lineage from Sureshwaracharya to the present day.

Dwaraka Sharada Peetham द्वारका शारदा पीठम्

Paschimamnaya · West

The western seat, located in the sacred city of Dwarka in Gujarat. Also known as the Kalika Matha, it stands in Krishna's city. The matha's own lineage begins with Hastamalakacharya (Sringeri's account names Padmapadacharya instead).

🟡 The Ashta Mathas of Udupi

Eight mathas established by Madhvacharya and his direct disciples to administer the Udupi Krishna Temple and propagate Dvaita philosophy. They rotate the privilege of presiding over the temple through the Paryaya system — each matha takes its turn for two years.

🔄 The Paryaya System

The eight Ashta Mathas do not each run the Udupi Krishna Temple independently. Instead, they rotate the responsibility through a system called Paryaya — each matha takes its turn as the temple's administrator for a period of two years. The handover ceremony takes place on 18 January of even-numbered years and is a major festival in Udupi.
