The Bhagavad Gita

Eighteen chapters, one conversation on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. Each chapter is a yoga — a path — unfolding from Arjuna's despair to Krishna's final call to surrender.

Despair Karma Yoga (Action) Jnana Yoga (Knowledge) Bhakti Yoga (Devotion) Dhyana Yoga (Meditation)
1

Arjuna Viṣāda Yoga अर्जुनविषादयोग

The Yoga of Arjuna's Despair

On the battlefield, Arjuna sees his teachers, cousins and friends arrayed against him. His bow slips from his hand, his mind reels, and he collapses in grief — refusing to fight.

😔 Viṣāda (Despair)47 verses
2

Sāṃkhya Yoga सांख्ययोग

The Yoga of Knowledge (Summary of the Gita)

Krishna begins his teaching: the soul is eternal, the body is temporary. Arjuna must do his duty without attachment to results. This chapter is often called the summary of the entire Gita.

🧠 Jnana72 versesKarma Yoga introduced
3

Karma Yoga कर्मयोग

The Yoga of Action

No one can remain truly inactive. The path is not to abandon action but to act without selfish attachment — as service, as sacrifice. Krishna explains the duties of one's station in life.

🔥 Karma43 verses
4

Jñāna Karma Sanyāsa Yoga ज्ञानकर्मसंन्यासयोग

The Yoga of Knowledge, Action & Renunciation

Krishna reveals his divine descent: he takes birth whenever dharma declines. He teaches that knowledge of the Self burns away all karma, and that the wise see action in inaction.

🧠 Jnana42 versesAvatāra teaching
5

Karma Sanyāsa Yoga कर्मसंन्यासयोग

The Yoga of Renunciation of Action

Arjuna asks: is it better to renounce action or to act? Krishna answers that both lead to the same goal — the key is inner detachment. The sage acts without being the doer.

🔥 Karma29 verses
6

Dhyāna Yoga ध्यानयोग

The Yoga of Meditation

Krishna describes the practice of meditation — posture, breath, withdrawal of the senses — and the difficulty of controlling the restless mind. The mind can be trained through practice and detachment.

🧘 Dhyana47 versesAṣṭāṅga Yoga
7

Jñāna Vijñāna Yoga ज्ञानविज्ञानयोग

The Yoga of Knowledge & Realization

Krishna describes his lower and higher natures, and the power of Māyā that veils him from the world. Four kinds of people turn to God; the wise, seeking the Self, are dearest.

🧠 Jnana30 versesMāyā
8

Aksara Brahma Yoga अक्षरब्रह्मयोग

The Yoga of the Imperishable Brahman

What happens at the moment of death? Krishna explains that the last thought determines the next birth — and that remembering him leads to liberation. The bright and dark paths of the soul are described.

🙏 Bhakti28 verses
9

Rāja Vidyā Yoga राजविद्यायोग

The Yoga of Royal Knowledge

Krishna reveals the "royal secret": he pervades all things, yet remains untouched. Even a leaf, a flower, or water offered with love is accepted. Devotion is the easiest and highest path.

🙏 Bhakti34 verses"Royal secret"
10

Vibhūti Yoga विभूतियोग

The Yoga of Divine Glories

Krishna describes his manifestations: he is the sun among lights, the Himalayas among mountains, the Ganges among rivers. Arjuna is overwhelmed and asks to see the full divine form.

🙏 Bhakti42 versesVibhūtis
11

Viśvarūpa Darśana Yoga विश्वरूपदर्शनयोग

The Yoga of the Vision of the Universal Form

Krishna grants Arjuna divine sight and reveals his cosmic form — all worlds, all beings, all time blazing in one infinite body. Arjuna trembles, terrified and awestruck, and begs Krishna to return to his gentle human form.

🙏 Bhakti55 versesCosmic vision
12

Bhakti Yoga भक्तियोग

The Yoga of Devotion

Arjuna asks: is it better to worship God with form or without form? Krishna describes the qualities of a true devotee — free from ego, equal in praise and blame, content, and devoted with unwavering love.

🙏 Bhakti20 versesQualities of a devotee
13

Kṣetra Kṣetrajña Vibhāga Yoga क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञविभागयोग

The Yoga of the Field & Its Knower

The body is the "field" (kṣetra); the consciousness that knows it is the "knower" (kṣetrajña). Krishna distinguishes matter from spirit, and teaches that the Self within all beings is the same.

🧠 Jnana35 verses
14

Guṇatraya Vibhāga Yoga गुणत्रयविभागयोग

The Yoga of the Three Guṇas

All of nature is woven from three qualities: sattva (clarity), rajas (passion), and tamas (inertia). These bind the soul to the body. One who transcends all three attains liberation.

🧠 Jnana27 versesThree guṇas
15

Puruṣottama Yoga पुरुषोत्तमयोग

The Yoga of the Supreme Person

The world is described as an inverted tree with roots above. Beyond the perishable and the imperishable stands the Supreme Person (Puruṣottama) — the source and support of all.

🧠 Jnana20 versesPuruṣottama
16

Daivāsura Sampad Vibhāga Yoga दैवासुरसम्पद्विभागयोग

The Yoga of Divine & Demoniac Natures

Krishna contrasts the divine qualities — fearlessness, purity, generosity — with the demoniac: arrogance, cruelty, greed. The demoniac are bound by desire and end in lower births.

🧠 Jnana24 verses
17

Śraddhātraya Vibhāga Yoga श्रद्धात्रयविभागयोग

The Yoga of the Threefold Faith

Faith, food, sacrifice and austerity are each of three kinds, corresponding to the three guṇas. Krishna explains that actions done without faith are worthless, and that "Om Tat Sat" sanctifies all offering.

🧠 Jnana28 verses
18

Mokṣa Sanyāsa Yoga मोक्षसंन्यासयोग

The Yoga of Liberation through Renunciation

Krishna summarizes all paths and gives his final teaching: abandon all dharma and take refuge in him alone. Arjuna rises, his delusion gone, and chooses to fight. Sanjaya rejoices.

🙏 Bhakti78 verses"Sarva-dharmān parityajya"
