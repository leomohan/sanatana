The Three Vedantas

Three great commentators on the Upanishads, three radically different visions of reality. All accept the same scriptures — yet they disagree on the most fundamental question: are we one with God, part of God, or eternally distinct from God?

📖 The Three Schools in Detail

1

Advaita अद्वैत

Absolute Non-dualism
"There is only one reality — and you are That."

Adi Shankara's Advaita is the most radical of the three. It argues that the seeming multiplicity of the world — you, me, God, the universe — is a superimposition on the single, undivided reality of Brahman. Liberation is not attaining something new but realizing what was always true: you were never separate from the infinite.

Key Teaching

Atman = Brahman. The individual self and ultimate reality are identical. The sense of being a separate person is the root ignorance (avidya).

2

Vishishtadvaita विशिष्टाद्वैत

Qualified Non-dualism
"One reality — but with real parts. You are part of God, not God."

Ramanuja rejected Shankara's maya, insisting that the world and souls are real. But they are not separate from God either: they are His body, inseparably related to Him as attributes to a substance. This is unity without identity — a middle path between merger and dualism.

Key Teaching

Unity in diversity. Brahman is the soul of the universe; individual souls and matter are its body. They are distinct yet inseparable — like the body and its indwelling self.

3

Dvaita द्वैत

Dualism
"God is God. You are you. And that difference is eternal."

Madhvacharya's Dvaita is the most emphatically theistic of the three. It insists that the distinction between God, souls, and the world is real and never overcome — even in liberation. God is supremely independent; everything else depends on Him utterly. The soul's joy lies in eternal service, not in becoming God.

Key Teaching

Eternal distinction. God (Vishnu), souls, and the material world are three eternally distinct realities. Liberation is eternal service, not merger.
