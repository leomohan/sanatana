📖 The Six Schools in Detail

c. 200 BCE – 200 CE

Nyaya न्याय

The School of Logic & Epistemology

Nyāya (“rule” or “method”) is India’s premier school of logic and epistemology. It systematically analyses how we know anything at all — defining four valid means of knowledge and a five-step syllogism that influenced centuries of debate. Later Nyāya developed a sophisticated theism, arguing for Īśvara as the intelligent cause of the world.

Founder
Gautama (Akṣapāda)
Core text
Nyāya Sūtra
Focus
Pramāṇa (means of knowledge), logic, debate
Key idea
Valid knowledge → liberation from suffering
⚖️ LogicEpistemology
c. 200 BCE – 100 CE

Vaisheshika वैशेषिक

The School of Categories & Atomism

Vaiśeṣika (“particularity”) is an early ontology — a systematic catalogue of everything that exists. Kaṇāda’s sūtras divide reality into six categories (padārtha), to which later writers added a seventh, non-existence (abhāva), and propose an atomistic physics in which material things arise from combinations of eternal atoms. It became the natural companion to Nyāya: ontology + logic.

Founder
Kaṇāda (Kaśyapa)
Core text
Vaiśeṣika Sūtra
Focus
Padārtha (categories of being), atomism
Key idea
Reality is analysable into six (later seven) categories
🌿 OntologyAtomism
c. 500 BCE – 500 CE

Samkhya सांख्य

The School of Dualism

Sāṃkhya (“enumeration”) is India’s oldest systematic philosophy. It posits two ultimate principles — Puruṣa (pure consciousness) and Prakṛti (matter/nature) — and explains the entire cosmos as the evolution of Prakṛti for the sake of Puruṣa. Liberation is kaivalya: realizing that consciousness was never bound in the first place.

Founder
Kapila
Core text
Sāṃkhya Kārikā (Īśvarakṛṣṇa)
Focus
Puruṣa vs. Prakṛti; 25 tattvas
Key idea
Discrimination (viveka) → kaivalya

c. 200 BCE – 400 CE

Yoga योग

The School of Practice

Patañjali’s Yoga is the practical counterpart to Sāṃkhya theory. Its 196 sūtras define yoga as citta-vṛtti-nirodha — the stilling of mental fluctuations — and lay out an eight-limbed path from ethics and posture to meditation and absorption. It remains India’s most globally influential philosophical export.

Founder
Patañjali
Core text
Yoga Sūtra (196 sūtras)
Focus
Aṣṭāṅga — eight limbs of practice
Key idea
Still the mind → kaivalya
🧘 PracticeMeditation
c. 300 BCE – 200 CE

Mimamsa मीमांसा

The School of Ritual Hermeneutics

Mīmāṃsā (“inquiry”) is the rigorous science of Vedic interpretation. Jaimini’s sūtras analyse the ritual injunctions of the Vedas with extraordinary precision, arguing that dharma is known only through scripture, and that right action — not metaphysical speculation — is the path to the good. It profoundly shaped Hindu law and ritual practice.

Founder
Jaimini
Core text
Mīmāṃsā Sūtra
Focus
Vedic ritual, dharma, hermeneutics
Key idea
Right action (karma) → svarga
🔥 RitualHermeneutics
c. 200 BCE – 800 CE

Vedanta वेदान्त

The School of the Upanishads

Vedānta (“end of the Vedas”) is the philosophical culmination of the Upanishads. Bādarāyaṇa’s Brahma Sūtra systematized its teachings, and later commentators — Śaṅkara (Advaita), Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita) and Madhva (Dvaita) — produced the schools that dominate Hindu thought today. Its central question is how the Self (Ātman) relates to ultimate reality (Brahman): identity for Advaita, inseparable relation for Viśiṣṭādvaita, eternal difference for Dvaita.

Founder
Bādarāyaṇa (Vyāsa)
Core text
Brahma Sūtra
Focus
Ātman, Brahman, mokṣa
Key idea
Knowledge of Brahman → liberation

🔗 The Three Pairs

The six schools are traditionally grouped into three complementary pairs — each pair combining theory with practice, or logic with ontology, or ritual with knowledge.

⚖️ Nyāya + Vaiśeṣika

Logic + Ontology. Nyāya supplies the tools of reasoning; Vaiśeṣika supplies the map of reality. Together they form a complete realist system — what exists, and how we can know it.

🧠 Sāṃkhya + Yoga

Theory + Practice. Sāṃkhya explains the metaphysics of consciousness and matter; Yoga provides the practical discipline to realize it. The Yoga Sūtra openly builds on Sāṃkhya categories.

🔥 Mīmāṃsā + Vedānta

Ritual + Knowledge. Mīmāṃsā focuses on the karma-kāṇḍa (action section) of the Vedas; Vedānta focuses on the jñāna-kāṇḍa (knowledge section). Together they cover the whole Vedic path.
