Temple Architecture

A Hindu temple is a house for a deity and a model of the cosmos, rising from a dark sanctum to a crowning finial. Texts on architecture (shilpa and vastu) name three great styles. Nagara is the style of the north, Dravida of the south, and Vesara is the hybrid of the Deccan between them.

Three ways to raise a tower

The styles differ most in the tower over the sanctum. The shape of the plan and the crown at the top follow from it.

📐 The texts disagree. The Manasara defines the three styles by the shape of the tower's crown: square for Nagara, octagonal for Dravida, round for Vesara. It also places them north, south and in between. The Kamikagama calls Vesara a Dravida plan with Nagara detail. Real temples mix freely. At Pattadakal the Chalukyas built northern and southern towers side by side.

The parts every temple shares

Garbhagriha

The "womb chamber", a small dark sanctum that holds the deity. Everything else is arranged around it.

Antarala & mandapa

A vestibule leads to one or more pillared halls, where devotees gather, dance and wait for darshan.

Pradakshina patha

The path for walking clockwise around the sanctum. It may be inside the walls or open to the sky.

Vastu-purusha mandala

Temple plans are laid out on a sacred grid, commonly of 8 × 8 = 64 squares, with Brahma at the centre over the sanctum.

Fourteen landmark temples

Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh

Nagara · Gupta, c. 500 CE

One of the earliest surviving stone temples of the north, with superb Vishnu panels. Its tower is mostly lost, but it shows the Nagara form beginning.

Kandariya Mahadeva, Khajuraho

Nagara · Chandela, c. 1030

The summit of the Khajuraho style. Its main spire is clustered with dozens of smaller spires (urushringas), so the whole temple rises like a mountain range.

Lingaraja, Bhubaneswar

Nagara (Kalinga) · 11th century

The Odisha school at its height. Its tall rekha deul (curved tower) stands over the sanctum, with a lower pyramidal pidha deul over the hall.

Sun Temple, Konark

Nagara (Kalinga) · c. 1250

Built by Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Gangas as the Sun god's chariot, with twenty-four carved wheels and seven horses. The main tower has fallen; the great hall still stands.

Sun Temple, Modhera

Nagara (Solanki) · 1026–27

A Chaulukya (Solanki) temple to Surya, fronted by a stepped tank whose flights of steps frame dozens of tiny shrines.

Shore Temple, Mamallapuram

Dravida · Pallava, early 8th century

Built under the Pallava king Rajasimha on the edge of the sea. It is one of the earliest structural (built, not rock-cut) stone temples in the south.

Kailasanatha, Kanchipuram

Dravida · Pallava, c. 700

Rajasimha's royal temple to Shiva. Its sandstone courtyard is lined with small cell-shrines, and it already has the stepped vimana and enclosure of the mature style.

Kailasa Temple, Ellora

Dravida · Rashtrakuta, 8th century

An entire Dravida temple carved downward out of a single hillside, with its courtyard, halls and vimana all cut from the living rock. It stands in Maharashtra, far north of the style's home.

Brihadeeswara, Thanjavur

Dravida · Chola, completed 1010

Rajaraja Chola's "great temple". Its vimana of about 66 m was the tallest of its day and is crowned by a single massive capstone. It is a World Heritage Site with Gangaikonda Cholapuram and Darasuram.

Meenakshi Amman, Madurai

Dravida · Nayaka, mostly 16th–17th century

The late Dravida city-temple, ringed by fourteen gopurams covered in painted stucco figures. The gateways now tower over the sanctum shrines.

Pattadakal

Vesara setting · Badami Chalukya, 7th–8th century

A royal temple group where the Chalukyas built in both styles side by side. The Virupaksha temple, raised by Queen Lokamahadevi, is Dravida; the Papanatha has a Nagara tower.

Chennakeshava, Belur

Vesara · Hoysala, 1117

Built by the Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana. It is famed for its bracket figures of dancers (madanikas) and its lathe-turned pillars, all carved in soft soapstone.

Hoysaleswara, Halebidu

Vesara · Hoysala, begun 1121

A twin temple whose outer walls carry continuous friezes of elephants, lions, horsemen and epic scenes, one above another.

Keshava, Somanathapura

Vesara · Hoysala, 1268

A small triple-shrine temple whose three star-shaped towers survive complete. It is the clearest picture of the finished Hoysala form.
