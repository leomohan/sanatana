The Shanmata

Six major sectarian traditions of Hinduism, each centered on a primary deity and a body of scripture. Traditionally attributed to Adi Shankara as a framework of worship — six paths, one ultimate reality. Not to be confused with the six darshanas (philosophical schools).

📖 The Six Traditions in Detail

🔱

Shaiva शैव

The Tradition of Shiva
🔱 Primary deity: Shiva

Shaivism is one of the largest and oldest Hindu traditions, worshipping Shiva as the supreme being — simultaneously destroyer and transformer, ascetic and householder. It spans a remarkable range: from the fierce Tamil bhakti of the Nayanmars to the sophisticated Kashmir Shaivism of Abhinavagupta, from the Virashaiva movement of Basava to the temple traditions of the 12 Jyotirlingas.

Key Teaching

Shiva as the ground of being. The Liṅga represents the formless infinite; the mantra Om Namah Śivāya is the five-syllable surrender to the auspicious one.

Deity
Shiva (with Parvati)
Texts
Śiva Purāṇa, Liṅga Purāṇa, Śiva Āgamas, Tirumurai
Acharyas
Nayanmars, Abhinavagupta, Basava, Appayya Dikshita
Festival
Maha Shivaratri, Pradosha, Karthigai Deepam
Mantra
Om Namah Śivāya

Vaishnava वैष्णव

The Tradition of Vishnu
🪷 Primary deity: Vishnu (and his avatars)

Vaishnavism is the largest sectarian tradition within Hinduism, centred on Vishnu and his incarnations — most beloved among them Rama and Krishna. Its devotional intensity finds expression in the Tamil hymns of the Azhwars, the Bengali ecstasy of Chaitanya, the philosophical rigor of Ramanuja and Madhva, and the childlike joy of the Bhagavata Purana.

Key Teaching

Descent for the sake of dharma. Vishnu takes birth whenever righteousness declines. The paths are bhakti (devotion), prapatti (surrender), and the chanting of the divine names.

Deity
Vishnu (with Lakshmi) — Rama, Krishna as avatars
Texts
Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Nālāyira Divya Prabandham
Acharyas
Azhwars, Ramanuja, Madhvacharya, Vallabha, Chaitanya
Festival
Vaikuntha Ekadashi, Rama Navami, Janmashtami
Mantra
Om Namo Nārāyaṇāya / Hare Krishna

Shakta शाक्त

The Tradition of the Goddess
🌺 Primary deity: Devi (the Goddess)

Shaktism worships the Divine as feminine — as Durga, Kali, Lakshmi, Saraswati, and countless other forms. It is the most ritually elaborate of the six traditions, with tantric practices, Sri Yantra meditation, and the great festival of Navaratri at its heart. The Devi Mahatmya celebrates her victory over the buffalo demon, and the Shakta Tantras elaborate her worship in extraordinary detail.

Key Teaching

The Goddess is ultimate reality. Śakti — divine power — is not subordinate to any god but is the very energy of the cosmos. She is both fierce and nurturing, both Kali and Lakshmi.

Deity
Devi — Durga, Kali, Lakshmi, Saraswati, and more
Texts
Devī Māhātmya, Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Tantras, Lalitā Sahasranāma
Acharyas
Ramakrishna, Bhaskararaya, Abhinavagupta, Krishnananda
Festival
Navaratri, Durga Puja, Diwali (Lakshmi Puja), Kali Puja
Mantra
Om Aim Hrīṃ Klīṃ Chāmuṇḍāyai Vicce

Ganapatya गाणपत्य

The Tradition of Ganesha
🐘 Primary deity: Ganesha

Ganapatya worship centers on Ganesha — the elephant-headed remover of obstacles, lord of beginnings, patron of arts and sciences. Though widely beloved across all Hindu traditions as the deity invoked first, the Ganapatya tradition elevates him to the supreme godhead. Its heartland is Maharashtra, where the eight Ashtavinayaka temples form a sacred circuit.

Key Teaching

Ganesha as the supreme. He is both the obstacle and its remover — the divine threshold. The Mudgala Purana narrates his eight incarnations, each overcoming a different inner demon.

Deity
Ganesha (with Buddhi and Siddhi)
Texts
Gaṇeśa Purāṇa, Mudgala Purāṇa, Gaṇeśa Gītā
Acharyas
Morya Gosavi, Chintamani Dev, the Mudgala lineage
Festival
Ganesha Chaturthi, Sankashti Chaturthi
Mantra
Om Gaṃ Gaṇapataye Namaḥ

Saura सौर

The Tradition of the Sun
☀️ Primary deity: Surya

Saura worship venerates Surya, the Sun, as the visible manifestation of the divine — the source of all life and light. Though fewer dedicated temples survive today, the tradition is ancient and widespread: the Konark Sun Temple, the Aditya Hridayam recited in the Ramayana, and the daily Surya Namaskara practiced across traditions all carry its imprint. Surya is invoked in the Gayatri Mantra, perhaps the most recited Vedic prayer.

Key Teaching

The visible divine. Surya is the light that sustains the world and the symbol of inner illumination. The Gayatri Mantra — the heart of Vedic prayer — is addressed to the solar light.

Deity
Surya (with Sanjñā and Chāyā)
Texts
Sūrya Upaniṣad, Āditya Hṛdayam, Sāmba Purāṇa, Sūrya Śataka
Acharyas
Samba, Mayūra, the Maga Brahmin tradition
Festival
Makara Sankranti, Ratha Saptami, Chhath Puja
Mantra
Om Sūryāya Namaḥ / Gāyatrī Mantra

🦚

Kaumara कौमार

The Tradition of Muruga
🦚 Primary deity: Muruga (Skanda/Kartikeya)

Kaumara worship is devoted to Muruga (also called Skanda, Kartikeya, Subrahmanya), the youthful son of Shiva and Parvati who commands the divine army and destroys evil. Its heartland is Tamil Nadu, where the six Arupadai Veedu temples trace his life, along with Sri Lanka and the Tamil diaspora, where the Tamil hymn traditions — Tiruppugal, Kanda Shashti Kavasam — sing his praises with extraordinary devotion.

Key Teaching

The divine commander. Muruga wields the vel (spear) of divine wisdom, which pierces ignorance and vanquishes darkness. He is both warrior and guru — the child who teaches the meaning of Om.

Deity
Muruga (with Deivanai and Valli)
Texts
Skanda Purāṇa, Kanda Shashti Kavasam, Tiruppugal, Thirumurugatruppadai
Acharyas
Arunagirinathar, Nakkirar, the Sangam poets
Festival
Skanda Sashti, Thaipusam, Panguni Uthiram
Mantra
Om Saravaṇabhavāya Namaḥ
