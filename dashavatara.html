Dashavatara

Whenever dharma declines, says the Gita, the Lord takes form. The ten best-known forms of Vishnu go from a fish in the flood to a rider still to come. Here they are in order, across the four ages of the world.

📿 Ten of many. The Bhagavata Purana (1.3) names twenty-two avatars and then says they are countless, like streams flowing from an inexhaustible lake. The ten below are the list that temple art, poetry and daily prayer settled on.

Satya Yuga
1

Matsya मत्स्य

The fish · saving life from the flood

A tiny fish begs Manu, the first man of this age, for protection, and grows until only the ocean can hold it. It warns of the coming flood. Manu builds a boat and ties it to the fish's horn, and the fish carries the sages and the seeds of all life to safety. Some Puranas add that Matsya recovered the Vedas, stolen by the demon Hayagriva.

Oldest form
The story of Manu and the fish is already told in the Shatapatha Brahmana.
Shrine
Vedanarayana temple, Nagalapuram, Andhra Pradesh
The floodManuThe Vedas recovered
2

Kurma कूर्म

The tortoise · the churning of the ocean

Gods and demons churn the ocean of milk for the nectar of immortality. Mount Mandara is the churning rod and the serpent Vasuki the rope. When the mountain begins to sink, Vishnu becomes a vast tortoise and bears it on his back. The churning brings up poison, which Shiva drinks, and then Lakshmi, the wish-granting cow and at last the amrita.

Shrine
Srikurmam, Srikakulam district, Andhra Pradesh — one of very few temples to Kurma
Samudra ManthanaMount MandaraAmrita
3

Varaha वराह

The boar · raising the earth

The demon Hiranyaksha drags the earth down into the cosmic waters. Vishnu takes the form of a boar, dives to the bottom, kills the demon and lifts the earth goddess Bhudevi on his tusks.

Oldest form
A boar that lifts the earth from the waters already appears in Vedic prose texts.
In stone
The colossal Varaha at the Udayagiri caves, Madhya Pradesh — early 5th century, Gupta period
Shrine
Bhuvarahaswamy temple, Srimushnam, Tamil Nadu
HiranyakshaBhudevi
4

Narasimha नरसिंह

The man-lion · protector of Prahlada

Hiranyakashipu, brother of Hiranyaksha, has a boon. He cannot be killed by man or beast, by day or by night, indoors or outdoors, on the ground or in the sky, by any weapon. He torments his son Prahlada for worshipping Vishnu. Vishnu bursts from a pillar as a being neither man nor beast. At dusk, on the threshold, he places the king on his lap and tears him apart with his claws.

Shrines
Ahobilam, Andhra Pradesh, with nine Narasimha shrines · Simhachalam, near Visakhapatnam
PrahladaThe pillarEvery condition kept
Treta Yuga
5

Vamana वामन

The dwarf · three steps

Bali, a generous asura king, has come to rule all three worlds. A dwarf brahmin boy asks him for as much land as three of his steps can cover. Bali agrees. The boy grows into Trivikrama, the giant of three strides, and covers earth and heaven in two steps. Bali offers his own head for the third, and is sent to rule the netherworld.

Oldest form
Vishnu who measures out the worlds in three strides is praised in the Rig Veda (1.154).
Festival
Onam in Kerala welcomes Mahabali on his yearly visit to his people
Shrines
Thrikkakara, Kerala · Ulagalantha Perumal temples at Kanchipuram and Thirukkoyilur
MahabaliTrivikramaOnam
6

Parashurama परशुराम

Rama with the axe

The son of the sage Jamadagni and Renuka. He carries an axe (parashu) that Shiva gave him. When King Kartavirya Arjuna seized his father's cow, Parashurama killed the king. When the king's sons killed Jamadagni, he cleared the earth of kshatriyas twenty-one times. He is counted among the chiranjivis, the deathless ones, and he appears in both epics. He meets Rama in the Ramayana and teaches Bhishma, Drona and Karna in the Mahabharata.

Legend
Throwing his axe into the sea, he raised the land of the western coast — Kerala and the Konkan
JamadagniKartaviryaChiranjivi
7

Rama राम

The prince of Ayodhya

The son of Dasharatha who keeps his father's word and spends fourteen years in exile. When Ravana carries off his wife Sita, he crosses to Lanka with an army of monkeys to bring her back. His reign became the measure of a just kingdom, Ram Rajya. Follow his route on the Ramayana journey map.

Dvapara Yuga
8

Krishna कृष्ण

Cowherd, king, charioteer

Born in a prison in Mathura and raised among the cowherds of Gokula. He kills his tyrant uncle Kamsa and builds Dvaraka by the sea. In the war of the Mahabharata he drives Arjuna's chariot and speaks the Bhagavad Gita. Many traditions see him as the source of all avatars rather than one of them.

In some lists
His elder brother Balarama, the plough-bearer, takes the eighth place. Krishna is then ninth, or stands above the list as its source.
GokulaDvarakaThe Gita
Kali Yuga · the present age
9

Buddha बुद्ध

The awakened one

Several Puranas include the Buddha. The Bhagavata Purana foretells his birth in Kikata (Magadha) as the son of Anjana. In Jayadeva's hymn he is praised for his compassion in condemning animal sacrifice. Other traditions leave him out and give the place to Balarama. Why he was included, and what it means, has long been debated.

Who keeps him
Most North Indian and many temple lists; Jayadeva's Gita Govinda
Who omits him
Sri Vaishnava and many South Indian lists
CompassionDisputed place
10

Kalki कल्कि

The rider to come

The only avatar still to come. At the end of the Kali Yuga, when dharma is all but lost, Kalki will ride out on a white horse with a blazing sword. He will end the age of strife and bring back an age of truth, and the cycle begins again. The Vishnu Purana names his birthplace as the village of Shambhala.

🧬 Since the late 19th century some writers have read the sequence as a parable of evolution: fish, amphibian, mammal, half-human, small human, then full humans. It is a modern reading. The Puranas present the avatars as rescues of dharma at moments of crisis, not as stages of biology.
