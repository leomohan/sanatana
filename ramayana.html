The Ramayana Journey

Rama's route from Ayodhya to Lanka and home again — thirteen stops placed where tradition places them, each with a note on how three great poets tell what happened there. Valmiki composed in Sanskrit, Kamban in Tamil, and Tulsidas in Awadhi; the same journey reads differently in each.

Bala KandaAyodhya KandaAranya KandaKishkindha KandaSundara KandaYuddha Kanda

Three poets, one road

Each poet follows the same route but stops at different places. Their differences are the subject of this map.

Valmiki · Sanskrit

The Adi Kavya, the first poem. About 24,000 verses in seven Kandas. Rama is an ideal man whose divinity the poem mostly leaves in the background. Scholars treat the Bala and Uttara Kandas as later layers.

Kamban · Tamil

The Iramavataram, usually dated to the 12th century. Over ten thousand verses in six Kandams, ending with the coronation. It is set in a Tamil landscape and is rich in emotion and drama. Rama is plainly God come down.

Tulsidas · Awadhi

The Ramcharitmanas, begun in Ayodhya in 1574. Seven Kands, told as Shiva tells it to Parvati. A work of bhakti from beginning to end; it is sung across North India and staged in every Ramlila.

🧭 The dashed line follows Rama's own route. Stop 10, Ashoka Vatika, is off the line: Hanuman went there alone and Rama never did. Most places on this map are traditional identifications. The epics name rivers, forests and hills, but not modern towns.

1

Ayodhya अयोध्या

Bala Kanda · The city of Kosala

The capital of Kosala, on the Sarayu. After a sacrifice for sons, King Dasharatha's three queens bear four princes. Kausalya bears Rama, Kaikeyi bears Bharata, and Sumitra bears the twins Lakshmana and Shatrughna. The sage Vishwamitra arrives and asks for young Rama to protect his sacrifice, and the journey begins.

Valmiki
Opens with Narada telling Valmiki of a perfect man. Grief at a hunter killing a bird turns into the first shloka.
Kamban
Opens slowly, with a river, a country and a city: long padalams describing the Sarayu, Kosala and Ayodhya.
Tulsidas
The whole story sits inside a series of dialogues. Shiva tells it to Parvati; other tellers pass it on.
DepartureDasharatha's four sons
2

Siddhashrama & the Tataka forest सिद्धाश्रम

Bala Kanda · Vishwamitra's hermitage

Vishwamitra leads the brothers east across the Ganga. On the way he teaches them the Bala and Atibala mantras against hunger and fatigue. Rama kills the demoness Tataka and receives celestial weapons. At Siddhashrama he guards the sage's sacrifice, driving off Maricha and killing Subahu. Maricha survives to return in the forest years.

Today
Buxar, Bihar — traditionally identified with Siddhashrama and the Tataka forest
TatakaFirst weaponsMaricha
3

Ahalya's hermitage अहल्या

Bala Kanda · Gautama's empty ashram

On the road to Mithila, Vishwamitra shows the brothers a deserted hermitage. Indra, disguised as the sage Gautama, had come to Gautama's wife Ahalya, and Gautama cursed them both. The three poets differ most clearly here.

Valmiki
She is not turned to stone. She lives unseen in the hermitage, fasting on air, until Rama arrives. She recognises Indra but consents. Rama touches her feet in respect.
Kamban
The curse turns her to stone. The dust of Rama's feet restores her. Vishwamitra remarks that he has seen the power of Rama's hand (against Tataka) and now the power of his feet.
Tulsidas
She has become stone. The touch of Rama's feet frees her, and she sings his praise. The stone form reaches Tulsidas through texts such as the Adhyatma Ramayana.
Today
Ahalya Sthan, Darbhanga district, Bihar (traditional)
AhalyaGautamaThree tellings
4

Mithila मिथिला

Bala Kanda · The bow and the wedding

King Janaka keeps Shiva's great bow. He has promised Sita, the daughter he found in a ploughed furrow, to whoever can string it. Rama lifts the bow and it breaks as he draws it. Four weddings follow. Rama marries Sita, Lakshmana marries Urmila, Bharata marries Mandavi and Shatrughna marries Shrutakirti. Parashurama then challenges Rama.

Valmiki
Rama and Sita do not meet before the bow. Parashurama confronts Rama on the road home to Ayodhya.
Kamban
They glimpse each other from a palace balcony and a street in Mithila, and fall in love before the bow is broken. This is the famous moment in which "he looked, and she looked".
Tulsidas
They first see each other in Janaka's flower garden (the Pushpavatika). Parashurama storms into the court as soon as the bow breaks.
Today
Janakpur, Nepal — traditionally Janaka's capital
Shiva's bowSitaParashurama
5

Shringaverapura शृङ्गवेरपुर

Ayodhya Kanda · Exile and the Ganga crossing

On the eve of Rama's coronation, Kaikeyi claims two old boons from Dasharatha. Bharata is to be king and Rama is to spend fourteen years in the forest. Rama, Sita and Lakshmana leave at once. At Shringaverapura the Nishada chief Guha receives them and they cross the Ganga. In Kamban, Rama embraces Guha as a brother. In Tulsidas, the boatman (kevat) insists on washing Rama's feet first. He fears that the dust which turned a stone into a woman might do the same to his boat.

Why exile — Valmiki
Manthara is alarmed by Rama's coronation and works on Kaikeyi until she gives way.
Why exile — Kamban
Manthara nurses an old grudge: as a boy, Rama hit her hump with a pellet from his toy bow.
Why exile — Tulsidas
The gods ask the goddess Saraswati to turn Manthara's mind, so that the exile serves the divine plan.
Today
Shringverpur, near Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh
Kaikeyi's boonsGuhaKevat
6

Chitrakoot चित्रकूट

Ayodhya Kanda · Bharata's visit

The exiles pass the hermitage of Bharadvaja at Prayag and settle on Chitrakoot hill. In Ayodhya, Dasharatha dies of grief. Bharata refuses the throne and comes to bring Rama back. Rama holds to his father's word. Bharata takes Rama's sandals (padukas), places them on the throne and rules as regent from Nandigram, outside Ayodhya.

All three
The padukas are among the best-loved images in every telling. Bharata's devotion is the counterweight to Kaikeyi's demand.
Today
Chitrakoot, on the Uttar Pradesh–Madhya Pradesh border
BharataPadukasDasharatha's death
7

Panchavati पञ्चवटी

Aranya Kanda · The golden deer

After years in the Dandaka forest and a visit to the sage Agastya, the three settle at Panchavati on the Godavari. Lakshmana mutilates Shurpanakha, Ravana's sister, and Rama destroys the army of Khara and Dushana. Ravana then sends Maricha as a golden deer. While Rama chases it, Ravana carries Sita off. The old vulture Jatayu fights him and falls.

The line — Valmiki
There is no Lakshmana rekha. Lakshmana leaves only after Sita's bitter accusations.
The line — Tulsidas
Not in his forest book either. Mandodari later taunts Ravana that he could not cross a line drawn by Lakshmana (Lanka Kand). The full scene belongs to later retellings.
The abduction — Valmiki
Ravana seizes Sita by her hair and thighs and lifts her into his chariot.
The abduction — Kamban
A curse forbids Ravana to touch a woman against her will, so he uproots the ground she stands on and carries it away.
The abduction — Tulsidas
Rama had already placed the real Sita in the keeping of Agni. Ravana takes only her shadow (the Maya Sita).
Today
Panchavati, Nashik, Maharashtra
ShurpanakhaGolden deerJatayu
8

Pampa & Shabari शबरी

Aranya Kanda · The ascetic who waited

Searching south, the brothers free the headless demon Kabandha, who sends them to Lake Pampa. There Shabari, the aged disciple of the sage Matanga, has waited years for Rama. She honours him and leaves her body.

Valmiki
She offers forest food gathered on the banks of the Pampa and rises to the heavens.
Kamban
A short episode: she is released from rebirth after seeing Rama.
Tulsidas
Rama teaches her the nine forms of devotion (navadha bhakti). It is one of the poem's best-loved passages.
The tasted berries
In none of the three. The scene of Shabari tasting each berry first comes from later devotional and folk retellings.
Today
Pampa Sarovar, near Hampi, Karnataka (traditional)
KabandhaMatangaNavadha bhakti
9

Kishkindha किष्किन्धा

Kishkindha Kanda · Hanuman, Sugriva and Vali

At Rishyamuka hill Hanuman meets the brothers and brings them to the exiled monkey prince Sugriva. Rama and Sugriva make a pact. Rama shoots Sugriva's brother Vali while the two brothers fight, and Sugriva is crowned. After the monsoon, search parties go out in every direction. In the south, the vulture Sampati, Jatayu's brother, sees Sita across the sea in Lanka.

Valmiki
Vali, struck from cover, rebukes Rama. Rama answers that Vali took his brother's wife and that Rama acts with the authority of the Ikshvaku kings.
Kamban
Vali's reproach is one of the poem's great speeches. Dying, he sees who Rama is and entrusts his son Angada to him.
Tulsidas
Vali questions Rama and accepts his answer. He dies at Rama's feet and gains liberation.
Today
Anegundi and Hampi, Karnataka — Anjanadri hill is revered as Hanuman's birthplace
HanumanSugrivaValiSampati
10

Ashoka Vatika, Lanka अशोकवाटिका

Sundara Kanda · Hanuman finds Sita

Hanuman leaps the ocean and searches Ravana's city by night. He finds Sita in a grove of ashoka trees, guarded by rakshasis and refusing Ravana. He gives her Rama's ring and takes her hair jewel back as a sign. Then he lets himself be captured, speaks to Ravana and sets Lanka on fire with his burning tail.

Valmiki
Searching the palace, Hanuman sees Mandodari asleep and for a moment takes her for Sita. He then rejects the thought.
Kamban
Back with Rama, Hanuman's first word is "I have seen" (kaṇḍēn). He says it first so that Rama's heart will not sink while he waits for news.
Tulsidas
Hanuman meets Vibhishana in Lanka; his house is marked by Rama's name and a tulasi plant.
Today
Seetha Amman Temple, Sita Eliya, near Nuwara Eliya, Sri Lanka. The identification is a modern tradition.
Rama's ringChudamaniLanka burns
11

Rameswaram & the Setu रामेश्वरम्

Yuddha Kanda · Vibhishana and the bridge

The army reaches the shore. Vibhishana, Ravana's brother, has warned Ravana to return Sita and been driven out. He crosses over and asks Rama for refuge. The ocean yields, and the monkey Nala leads the building of the bridge to Lanka.

Valmiki
Rama's vow of refuge: to anyone who comes even once and says "I am yours", he gives safety from all beings.
Kamban
Rama tells Vibhishana that with Guha they became five brothers, with Sugriva six, and with him seven.
Tulsidas
Before the bridge is built Rama installs and worships a Shiva lingam. Valmiki only has Rama point out the place on the flight home.
Today
Ramanathaswamy Temple, Rameswaram, and Dhanushkodi, Tamil Nadu
VibhishanaNalaRama Setu
12

The war in Lanka लङ्का

Yuddha Kanda · Ravana falls

The siege brings the great duels. Kumbhakarna wakes to fight and falls. Indrajit binds the brothers with serpent-arrows. When Lakshmana falls to a mighty weapon, Hanuman carries back a whole mountain of herbs to revive him. At last Rama kills Ravana. Then comes Sita's ordeal by fire.

The fire — Valmiki
Rama speaks harshly to Sita in front of the army. She enters the fire, and Agni gives her back unharmed.
The fire — Kamban
Sita enters the fire in the same way, and the fire god himself returns her and declares her purity.
The fire — Tulsidas
Only the shadow Sita enters the fire. The real Sita, kept safe by Agni since Panchavati, returns.
Today
Divurumpola, near Welimada, Sri Lanka, is the traditional site of the fire ordeal
KumbhakarnaIndrajitSanjivaniAgni pariksha
13

Nandigram & the coronation नन्दिग्राम

Yuddha Kanda · Home by the Pushpaka

Rama flies home in the aerial car Pushpaka, pointing out each place to Sita on the way. Hanuman goes ahead to Bharata at Nandigram, where Bharata had kept the sandals on the throne for fourteen years. The brothers meet, and Rama is crowned in Ayodhya.

Valmiki
The Yuddha Kanda ends with the coronation. The Uttara Kanda adds Sita's banishment and her return to the earth; scholars widely see it as a later layer.
Kamban
Ends with the coronation. A Tamil Uttara Kandam is attributed to Ottakoothar, not Kamban.
Tulsidas
The Uttar Kand describes Rama's reign (Ram Rajya) and the crow-sage Kakabhushundi's teaching. It does not tell of Sita's exile.
Today
Nandigram (Bharat Kund), south of Ayodhya
PushpakaBharataPattabhishekam
