Shakti Peethas

The seats of the Goddess, each marking where a part of Sati's body — or one of her ornaments — is said to have fallen. This map follows the eighteen Maha Shakti Peethas of the hymn traditionally attributed to Adi Shankara, from Sri Lanka to Kashmir, and adds the four Adi ("first") Peethas.

18 Maha Shakti PeethasAdi Shakti Peethas

The legend of Sati

Daksha Prajapati held a great sacrifice and pointedly left out his daughter Sati and her husband Shiva. Sati went anyway, was insulted by her father before the assembly, and gave up her body — in some tellings by entering the sacrificial fire, in others through yogic power. Shiva, in fury, sent Virabhadra to destroy the sacrifice.

Grief-stricken, Shiva wandered the cosmos carrying Sati's body. In one version Vishnu cut it into pieces with his Sudarshana Chakra; in another the limbs and ornaments simply fell as Shiva walked. Wherever a part came to rest, the Goddess is worshipped — usually alongside a guardian form of Shiva called Bhairava.

Shankari शाङ्करी

Trincomalee, Sri Lanka

The first name in the stotra, "Lankayam Shankari Devi". The original site is historically unknown; modern tradition places it at the clifftop Koneswaram temple in Trincomalee, destroyed by the Portuguese in the 17th century and rebuilt in the 20th.
