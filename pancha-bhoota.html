Pancha Bhoota Sthalams

Five Shiva temples in South India, each worshipped as one of the five elements (pancha bhuta) from which the world is made: earth, water, fire, air and space. Four are in Tamil Nadu; the air temple, Srikalahasti, is in Andhra Pradesh.

1

Earth · Ekambareswarar நிலம் · ஏகாம்பரேஸ்வரர், காஞ்சிபுரம்

Prithvi Lingam — Kanchipuram

Parvati is said to have shaped a lingam of sand under a mango tree on the banks of the Vegavati and, when the river flooded, held it in her embrace to protect it. Shiva appeared as Ekambareswarar, "Lord of the mango tree". Because the lingam is of earth, the usual water abhishekam is not performed on it. The temple's ancient mango tree has four branches said to stand for the four Vedas, and the complex even holds a Divya Desam of Vishnu.

Water · Jambukeswarar நீர் · ஜம்புகேஸ்வரர், திருவானைக்காவல்

Appu Lingam — Thiruvanaikaval, Tiruchirappalli

An underground spring keeps the lingam in the low sanctum partly under water. Akilandeswari is said to have fashioned it from Kaveri water under a jambu (naval) tree. The temple's name recalls an elephant (aanai) that bathed the lingam each day and a spider that spun a web to shade it; tradition holds the spider was reborn as the Chola king Kochengat, credited with building the shrine. It stands just across the river from Srirangam.

Fire · Annamalaiyar நெருப்பு · அண்ணாமலையார், திருவண்ணாமலை

Agni Lingam — Tiruvannamalai

Shiva once appeared as an endless column of fire whose top and bottom Brahma and Vishnu could not find; the red hill of Arunachala behind the temple is worshipped as that pillar, and as Shiva himself. On Karthigai Deepam a great beacon is lit on the summit, and on every full moon pilgrims walk the roughly 14 km path around the hill (girivalam).

Air · Srikalahasteeswara காற்று · காளத்திநாதர், திருக்காளத்தி

Vayu Lingam — Srikalahasti, Andhra Pradesh

A lamp in the sanctum is seen to flicker constantly though no breeze enters — taken as the presence of Vayu. The town's name joins Sri (spider), Kala (serpent) and Hasti (elephant), three creatures said to have worshipped the lingam. It is also where the hunter Kannappa Nayanar offered his own eyes to Shiva, and it is visited as a Rahu–Ketu kshetra.

Space · Nataraja ஆகாயம் · நடராஜர், சிதம்பரம் (தில்லை)

Akasha Lingam — Chidambaram

Here Shiva is worshipped chiefly as Nataraja, performing the Ananda Tandava, rather than as a lingam. Beside him, behind a curtain, hangs a garland of golden bilva leaves before what appears to be empty space: the Chidambara Rahasyam, the "secret of Chidambaram", which represents Shiva as formless space. For Tamil Saivas this is simply Koil, "the temple".

A curious alignment

Three of the five — Srikalahasti (air), Kanchipuram (earth) and Chidambaram (space) — lie within about a hundredth of a degree of the same longitude, close to 79.7°E, across some 260 km from north to south. Visitors often remark on it; the texts that praise these temples do not mention it.

All five are also Paadal Petra Sthalams, sung by the Nayanmars in the Tevaram — see the Azhwars & Nayanmars and Shiva & Muruga pages.
